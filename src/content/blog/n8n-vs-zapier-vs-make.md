These three tools do the same basic job: connect your apps, wait for something to happen, then do a chain of things in response. The differences show up when your workflows stop being simple.

We should declare our bias up front. We build a lot in n8n. That is because of how our clients' work tends to turn out, not because the others are bad, and we will say so when they are the better fit.

## The short version

| If you… | Lean towards |
| --- | --- |
| Want the quickest start and mostly link popular apps | Zapier |
| Want a visual builder with more control over branching and data than the simplest tools give you | Make |
| Need custom code, AI steps, complex logic or control over where it runs | n8n |
| Have no one to maintain it and a simple need | Whichever you find easiest, and keep it small |

## Zapier

Zapier is a hosted service with a very large catalogue of app connections and a gentle learning curve. For straightforward "when this happens, do that" jobs it is hard to beat for speed. The trade-off tends to show up when workflows get long or branching, and when you want to do something the ready-made connections do not cover.

## Make

Make is also hosted, with a visual canvas that makes the flow of data easier to see and gives you more control over branching and handling lists of items. It rewards people who are comfortable thinking in terms of data and structure.

## n8n

n8n is a workflow automation platform that supports custom code, AI steps and complex logic, and it can be used as a cloud service or self-hosted. That flexibility is the reason we use it. The trade-off is that it asks more of whoever builds and maintains it, which is a good argument for having someone who does this every day look after it.

## How to choose

Ask these questions in order:

1. **How complicated will this get?** If the honest answer is "two apps and one step", pick the easiest tool and move on.
2. **Will it need AI, custom code or an unusual integration?** If yes, you want a tool that does not stop you at the edge of its ready-made options.
3. **Does it matter where your data goes?** If you have reasons to control where information is processed, check how each tool handles that.
4. **Who will look after it?** A workflow that nobody understands is a liability on any platform.
5. **What will it cost at your volumes?** Each tool prices differently and changes its plans from time to time. Check current pricing for your expected usage before you commit, rather than relying on a comparison written months ago.

## Switching later

It is possible to move workflows between platforms, but it is rarely a copy and paste. It means rebuilding and retesting. If you expect your needs to grow, factor that in when you start.
